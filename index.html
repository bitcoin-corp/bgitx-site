How it works

Six steps from repo to shipped.

Your code stays yours. bGitX adds a room, the people, the terms and a builder that asks before it merges.

  1. 1

    Connect your GitHub

    Pick the repos b can see. Nothing else is touched. You can disconnect any time.

    Live
  2. 2

    Mint your repo

    A snapshot of the repo will be sealed on chain, encrypted to your keys and private by default. Showing it to anyone will be a separate step you choose.

    Coming
  3. 3

    Open a room for it

    Each repo gets its own room in bChatX. Talk about the code where the code lives.

    Live
  4. 4

    Invite collaborators

    Bring in the people who work on it. A repo token records who owns and worked on the code, and gives access to the room.

    Testing
  5. 5

    Agree terms with bSignX

    Who did what, who can merge, who gets access. Written down and signed by everyone in the room before work starts.

    Testing
  6. 6

    Kintsugi fixes and builds

    Report a bug or ask for a feature. Kintsugi works in a branch and opens a pull request. You review it. Nothing merges without your OK.

    Live

Nothing merges without your OK. Kintsugi works on a branch and opens a pull request. You read the diff and the test results, and only your OK lands it.

Your OK, every time

A builder that asks first.

Kintsugi is the repair engine behind b. It does the work. You keep the say.

Pull requests, not pushes

Every change arrives as a pull request on a branch. You read the diff and the test results before anything lands.

Nothing merges without your OK

No silent commits to main. No surprise deploys.

Narrow access

Kintsugi sees only the repos you pick. It holds no production secrets, no deploy rights and no wallet keys.

No passwords in chat

We never ask for your passwords or keys. Secrets stay in your hosting, set by you.

Private by default

When snapshots arrive, each one will be encrypted to your keys. Making one public will be a separate, deliberate step.

Security reports stay private

They skip the queue and are never shown in a room.
